About

{{ app_name }}

A study app for remembering what you learn. Recognising an answer isn't the same as knowing it, so instead of flipping cards you explain each answer in your own words and get feedback on how much of it you really retained.

The name

The name comes from a parable in the Zhuangzi: a wheelwright named Bian tells Duke Huan that the books he's reading are just the dregs of men long dead, because the real skill, how hard to chisel, how loose to cut, lives only in the hand.

While flashcards and spaced repetition are useful tools, this app is a reminder that no amount of memorization replaces that: real mastery comes from experience.

How it works

  • Explain, don't flip. You type the answer in your own words, and Claude compares it with the reference: how much you retained, what you missed or got wrong, and a tighter way to say it.
  • Forgotten to retained. That assessment sets a slider, which you can adjust, and it decides when you see the card again.
  • Spaced repetition. The FSRS scheduler brings each card back just before you're likely to forget it, so the gaps grow as the memory gets stronger.
  • Fresh numbers. Maths cards change their numbers every time, so you practise the method rather than memorising an answer.
